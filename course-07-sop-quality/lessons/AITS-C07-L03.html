Quality assurance and completion review

Sample Content — Pending Company Approval

Version 0.1.0 | AITS-C07-L03 | Draft | 15 minutes

Learning objective

Distinguish process prevention from checking a finished result.

Explanation

Quality assurance builds reliable methods, training and review into the work. Quality control checks outputs against defined requirements. Both are useful: a final inspection can detect a defect, while a better preparation process may prevent it. The ISO overview supports this general distinction; this course makes no claim that AITS holds a particular certification.

For a completion review, compare the approved scope with evidence and open issues. Confirm that required tests have results, exceptions have an authorized disposition and the handover is understandable. Record recurring problems for improvement without changing a controlled procedure on your own. A customer signature alone does not erase an unresolved safety or technical issue.

Synthetic example

Repeated wrong device quantities suggest a preparation and reconciliation problem, even if a final count catches each error.

Practical exercise

Propose one preventive process check and one final output check for an installation kit.

Model response

Preventive: reconcile approved scope and picking list before dispatch. Output: verify actual kit quantities and condition against the list.

Summary

QA improves the process QC checks the output Close work against evidence and scope
